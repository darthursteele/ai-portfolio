# Stage 6: Headline Brainstorm

## Purpose

The resume headline is the first thing a hiring manager reads. It sets the frame for everything that follows. A generic headline costs nothing — and does nothing. The goal here is a headline that signals the exact persona the hiring manager is looking for, in language that sounds like a person rather than a LinkedIn template.

## Inputs

- Approved positioning strategy from Stage 5
- JD language (especially how the role is framed, what the company values)
- Gap analysis from Stage 3 (what to foreground, what to background)
- Candidate's most distinctive credentials

## Process

**Step 1:** State the current headline and give a hiring-manager-eye reaction in 2–3 sentences. What does it signal? What does it miss? Does it help or hurt this specific application?

**Step 2:** Identify the three qualities the winning candidate must signal in their headline for this specific role.

**Step 3:** Generate five alternative headlines using distinct literary devices:
1. **Rule of three** — three parallel phrases that build
2. **Juxtaposition** — two things that don't obviously go together but do in this candidate's case
3. **Paradox** — a statement that seems contradictory but resolves in the candidate's favor
4. **Specificity** — a concrete, verifiable claim that implies the rest
5. **Contrast** — before/after, then/now, this-not-that

For each headline:
- Show the headline
- One sentence explaining the strategic logic (why this framing, what it signals to this hiring manager)

**Step 4:** Ask the user to pick one, request changes, or say they want another round. Iterate until they approve. Explicitly confirm the approved headline before moving to Stage 7.

## Standards

- Maximum ~15 words; shorter is usually better
- Avoid: "results-driven," "strategic," "passionate," "innovative," "dynamic"
- Avoid: title repetition (don't just restate the job title they're applying for)
- The headline should work for this role specifically, not as a generic personal brand
- It should sound like something a smart person wrote, not a template filled in
